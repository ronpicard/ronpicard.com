Session 5 of Software Lessons, “Networking 102: TCP, UDP and Bandwidth,” runs about 35 minutes.

What this session covers

  • IP packets and data flow
  • ISPs, download versus upload speeds
  • Signal waves
  • Ping versus bandwidth
  • Transport and delivery
    • UDP
    • TCP
    • Broadcast
    • Multicast

Key ideas

  • Data crosses a network as packets, small chunks that each carry an address and may take different routes.
  • Bandwidth is how much data can be sent per unit of time, while ping measures how long a round trip takes, so a connection can have high bandwidth and still be slow to respond.
  • TCP sets up a connection and retransmits lost data so it arrives complete and in order.
  • UDP sends packets without that checking, which is faster and suits uses where speed matters more than perfect delivery.
  • Broadcast sends to every device on a network, and multicast sends to a chosen group of receivers.
