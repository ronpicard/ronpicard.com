Domino Topple is a 3D chain-reaction puzzle set on tabletops in three furnished rooms: a study with a desk lamp and bookshelf, a garage workshop with a pegboard of tools, and a pastel playroom strewn with toys. Every level has a trigger, such as a swinging pendulum, a wind-up car or a marble chute, and a goal, such as a bell, a flag or a cup. You build the chain between them from dominoes, stairs, bridges, levers, marbles, ramps and springs, then press GO and watch the physics play out.

Drag across the table to lay a whole row of glossy ivory dominoes at once, turn or remove pieces from the buttons that appear above them, and replay a run in slow motion. Fifteen levels award up to three stars each for reaching the goal, beating par and knocking over the bonus star, and a sandbox hands you the full set of pieces. It works with mouse, keyboard and touch on desktops and phones.

How it works

Every run is a real rigid-body simulation with Rapier, in centimetres and seconds, stepped at a fixed 120 steps a second. Dominoes are solid blocks with real mass and friction. The pendulum, lever and bridge swing on hinges, and marbles use continuous collision detection so they never tunnel through a thin domino. A run succeeds the moment the goal is reached, and fails once everything has come to rest without reaching it, or after 40 seconds.

The browser and the test suite use the same simulation code, and a run with the same layout always plays out the same way. Every level ships with a reference solution, and the tests run it headlessly and check that it earns all three stars and that the level cannot be finished from an empty table.

Game logic is kept apart from rendering and input. The piece catalogue, levels, editor, placement rules and scoring are in src/game/, and the react-three-fiber scene is in src/scene/, using instanced dominoes so a long row costs one draw call. Graphics come in three tiers, and on Auto the game measures its own frame time when a level opens and picks one. Sound effects are synthesised in code at runtime.

Controls

  • Tap the table to place a piece, or drag across it to lay a row. Tap a placed piece to select it, then turn or remove it from the buttons above it or drag it to move it.
  • GO runs the physics, STOP returns to building with your layout intact, and the half-speed button plays the run in slow motion.
  • Keys 1 to 8 select a dock piece, V is the Hand tool, and 0 or Esc deselects.
  • Arrow keys nudge the preview or selected piece, Enter places it, Q and E rotate by 15 degrees, R rotates by 90, and Delete removes it.
  • Ctrl or Cmd with Z undoes, with Shift redoes. Space is GO or STOP, S is slow motion.
  • Bracket keys turn the view, plus and minus zoom, T is the top view, C resets the camera, and H opens help.
  • On a touch screen, drag with two fingers to slide the view and pinch to zoom.

Built with

React, TypeScript, react-three-fiber, drei and Three.js, with postprocessing for ambient occlusion and bloom, Rapier for physics, Web Audio, and Vite. Progress and settings are kept in your browser.
